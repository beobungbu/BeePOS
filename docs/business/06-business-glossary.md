# BeePOS Retail Business Glossary

## Purpose

This glossary standardizes business language used across research, SOPs, requirements and future product discussions.

## A

### Allocation
A business process that assigns available or incoming inventory to stores / locations based on demand, plan, priority or rules.

### Assortment
The set of products a retailer decides to offer for a store, store cluster, channel, category or period.

### Average Basket Value (ABV)
Net sales divided by number of sales transactions, subject to a governed definition of net sales.

## B

### Backorder
Ordered quantity that cannot be fulfilled on the original expected schedule and remains outstanding for later fulfillment.

### Business Day
The retailer-defined operating date used for sales / cash / reporting. It may not always equal calendar date, especially for stores operating past midnight.

## C

### Category
A commercial grouping of merchandise managed with common customer need, assortment, pricing, promotion and performance objectives.

### Cycle Count
A planned physical count of a subset of inventory instead of counting the entire store / warehouse.

### Cash Over / Short
Difference between actual counted cash and expected cash for a till / shift / business day.

## D

### Days of Supply
Estimated number of days current inventory will cover expected demand.

### Delivery Note
Supplier / carrier document accompanying delivered goods, usually stating shipment contents and quantities.

### Discount
A reduction from an otherwise applicable selling amount. Business analysis should distinguish manual discount, promotion benefit, coupon, markdown and price-list selection.

## E

### Effective Date
Date/time from which a price, promotion, assortment or other commercial rule becomes applicable.

### FEFO — First Expired, First Out
Stock-rotation principle prioritizing the earliest-expiring inventory for sale / issue.

### FIFO — First In, First Out
Stock-rotation or costing concept based on oldest receipt first. Operational FIFO and accounting FIFO should not be assumed to be identical without policy definition.

## G

### Goods Receipt
Business record that goods were physically received and accepted, wholly or partially, at a destination.

### Gross Margin
Sales less cost of goods sold, with exact tax / discount / cost treatment defined by finance policy.

## I

### Inventory Accuracy
Degree to which system-recorded stock matches physical stock.

### Inventory Adjustment
Authorized correction to recorded inventory due to count variance, damage, loss, expiry or another documented reason.

### In-Transit Inventory
Goods dispatched from a source but not yet accepted at destination.

## L

### Lead Time
Elapsed time between a defined starting point (for example PO placement) and fulfillment / receipt.

### Loyalty Redemption
Use of accumulated customer loyalty value such as points or benefits to reduce payable value or receive a reward.

## M

### Markdown
Planned reduction in regular selling price, often used for clearance, lifecycle, seasonal or aging-stock management.

### Merchandise Hierarchy
Structured grouping of products used for planning and reporting, such as Division → Department → Category → Subcategory.

### MOQ — Minimum Order Quantity
Minimum quantity a supplier or business rule permits / economically requires for an order.

## O

### On-Hand
Recorded physical inventory present at a location, subject to the retailer's exact status definition.

### On-Shelf Availability
Whether a customer can actually find the intended product available for purchase on the shelf, not merely whether inventory exists somewhere in the store.

### OOS — Out of Stock
Condition where required saleable stock is unavailable. A retailer should distinguish system OOS from shelf OOS.

### OTIF — On Time In Full
Supplier / logistics service metric measuring delivery that is both on time and in the required quantity.

## P

### Pack Size
Number / quantity of base selling or inventory units contained in a purchase / handling pack.

### Paid-In
Authorized addition of cash to a till other than normal sales receipts.

### Paid-Out
Authorized removal of cash from a till for an approved business reason.

### PO — Purchase Order
Formal commercial order sent to a supplier specifying goods, quantities, terms, destination and expected delivery.

### Price Override
Authorized transaction-level or local replacement of the normally applicable selling price.

### Promotion
Time / condition-based commercial offer that grants a defined benefit to eligible transactions or customers.

### Purchase Price Variance
Difference between an expected / standard / prior purchase cost and actual purchasing cost under the retailer's finance definition.

## R

### Replenishment
Process of determining and fulfilling additional stock needs at a store / warehouse.

### Reorder Point
Inventory threshold at which replenishment is triggered under a reorder-point method.

### Return
Customer or supplier-facing reversal / movement where previously sold or purchased goods are sent back according to policy.

### Return Disposition
Decision on what happens to returned goods: saleable stock, quarantine, supplier return, damage/write-off or other destination.

### Retail Transaction
Completed or controlled in-progress business interaction at retail, such as sale, return, exchange or void, depending on process definition.

## S

### Safety Stock
Additional stock held to buffer uncertainty in demand or supply.

### Sell-In
Sales from manufacturer / supplier to distributor / dealer / retailer, depending on channel context.

### Sell-Out
Sales from downstream channel participant to the next stage / final consumer, depending on channel context.

### Shelf Gap
Expected product position on shelf is empty or unavailable to the customer.

### Shrinkage
Inventory loss not explained by legitimate sales / known movements, typically from theft, damage, process error or administrative error.

### SKU — Stock Keeping Unit
Distinct stockable / sellable merchandise item used for inventory and commercial operations.

### Stock Count
Physical inventory counting process used to compare actual quantity with recorded quantity.

### Stock Transfer
Controlled movement of inventory between internal locations such as warehouse-to-store or store-to-store.

### Supplier Fill Rate
Quantity supplied divided by quantity ordered / due, according to the chosen measurement definition.

## T

### Tender
Form of value used to settle a sale, e.g. cash, card, bank transfer / QR, voucher or points where policy permits.

### Till / Register
Physical or logical checkout station used by a cashier to perform sales and handle tenders.

### Target Stock
Desired inventory level used by replenishment policy.

## U

### UOM — Unit of Measure
Unit in which a quantity is expressed, such as each, pack, case, kilogram or liter.

### Units per Transaction (UPT)
Number of sold units divided by number of sales transactions under the governed business definition.

## V

### Void
Cancellation of a transaction or line before / according to controlled transaction-finalization policy. It should be distinguished from a post-sale return/refund.

## W

### Working Capital
Capital tied in short-term operating assets and liabilities; retail analysis commonly considers inventory, receivables and payables.

### WOS — Weeks of Supply
Inventory coverage expressed in weeks based on forecast / consumption.

## Z

### Z Report / End-of-Day Report
Retail term commonly used for finalized register / business-day sales and tender summary. Exact legal and operational meaning varies by market and operating policy.

---

## Document terminology principles

1. Do not use `order` without context. Distinguish Purchase Order, Sales Order, Customer Order and Retail Transaction.
2. Do not use `stock` as a single ambiguous quantity. State on-hand, available, reserved, in-transit, damaged, etc.
3. Do not use `discount` to mean every price reduction. Distinguish pricing, markdown, promotion, coupon, loyalty and manual override.
4. Do not use `return` to mean only refund. Return includes eligibility, commercial settlement and physical disposition.
5. Define KPI formulas before using KPI names in executive reporting.

# P23 — Customer Analytics-to-Targeting

Status: SOP-level business process specification · desk-research baseline

## 1. Purpose

Turn consented customer and transaction data into actionable segments, targeted offers and measurable retention/growth programs without obscuring privacy or commercial accountability.

## 2. Scope

Includes:

- customer data quality/readiness;
- segmentation;
- behavioral/loyalty analysis;
- target audience definition;
- offer/campaign selection;
- suppression/exclusion;
- campaign execution handoff;
- response measurement;
- incremental-effect review;
- customer fatigue/abuse monitoring.

## 3. Actors

CRM/Loyalty, Marketing, Category/Commercial, Data/Analytics, Customer Service, Privacy/Compliance, Finance.

## 4. Evidence / inputs

- customer profile and consent;
- purchase/return history;
- loyalty earn/redeem history;
- campaign exposure/response;
- customer service history;
- channel preference;
- product/category affinity;
- margin/offer cost;
- suppression/opt-out lists.

## 5. Segmentation flow

1. confirm valid data scope and consent basis;
2. define business question/objective;
3. select relevant measures and time window;
4. create segment criteria;
5. validate segment size/quality;
6. exclude ineligible/opted-out/high-risk customers;
7. review commercial economics;
8. approve audience/offer;
9. publish audience to campaign channel;
10. measure response and incrementality where practical;
11. update learning and future targeting rules.

## 6. Common segment dimensions

- recency/frequency/value;
- category/product affinity;
- store/channel preference;
- loyalty tier;
- lapsed/new/active status;
- promotion sensitivity;
- return/refund pattern;
- geographic/store catchment;
- B2B account/dealer type where applicable.

## 7. Targeted offer principles

- offer has explicit objective;
- audience and eligibility are documented;
- expected cost/margin impact is estimated;
- incompatible promotions are considered;
- frequency/contact limits respected;
- result measured against meaningful comparator/baseline where possible.

## 8. Exceptions

- missing/invalid consent;
- duplicated customer identity;
- customer merged/split incorrectly;
- offer sent after opt-out;
- audience too small/large;
- campaign causes margin dilution without incremental sales;
- repeated reward abuse;
- high return/refund behavior distorts customer value.

## 9. Controls

- consent/contact preference checked before activation;
- suppression list enforced;
- customer segments versioned/dated;
- manual audience override reason-coded;
- financial impact reviewed for material offers;
- loyalty/customer data access restricted;
- sensitive traits not inferred/used outside approved policy/legal basis.

## 10. KPIs

- active customer rate;
- repeat purchase rate;
- retention/reactivation;
- campaign conversion;
- incremental revenue/margin;
- redemption rate;
- cost per incremental order;
- unsubscribe/opt-out rate;
- customer contact frequency;
- loyalty participation/tier migration.

## 11. Worked example

A segment of “high-value lapsed customers” should not be defined only by historical spend. Returns, recency, margin contribution, opt-out status and recent service complaints may materially change whether a customer belongs in a win-back offer.

## 12. Research anchors

- Oracle Retail Customer Engagement / loyalty program concepts.
- APQC customer/marketing process definitions and measures.

## 13. Open retailer-policy questions

- approved segmentation dimensions;
- inactivity/lapse definition;
- marketing consent rules;
- contact-frequency cap;
- campaign approval threshold;
- incrementality measurement method;
- high-return/abuse handling.
